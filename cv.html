Research Experience

Systematic Analysis of Solar Active Region NOAA AR 12781 Manuscript in Progress
  • Aim: Investigated the relationship between magnetic flux evolution and coronal brightness using multi-wavelength SDO (AIA/HMI) and Parker Solar Probe observations.
  • Methodology: Developed a SunPy-based analysis pipeline, applied manual contour tracking for spatial consistency, and incorporated a 3-day temporal offset to compare solar observations with OMNI solar wind measurements.
  • Result: Confirmed an inverse correlation between magnetic flux and coronal intensity during the active region decay phase.
Comprehensive Analysis of 501 Stars and Their Exoplanetary Systems MAST Database Study
  • Conducted a systematic analysis of 501 stars selected from the MAST database to search for exoplanet transit signals and cross-match candidates with confirmed host stars.
  • Utilized the Lightkurve Python package to process and analyze stellar light curves obtained from Kepler and TESS observations.
  • Extracted stellar and planetary parameters from the NASA Exoplanet Archive and performed statistical comparisons and HR diagram visualizations to study stellar properties that favor planet hosting.
Morphological Analysis of a Galaxy IUCAA Workshop | Nirmala College
  • Analyzed HST FITS imaging using SAOImage DS9 to characterize galaxy morphology and construct a growth curve.
  • Derived an angular radius of 2.4 arcseconds and physical radius of 15.3 kpc from background-subtracted photometry and redshift data.
  • Calculated photometric properties: Apparent Magnitude ≈ 21.06 and Absolute Magnitude ≈ -21.39, suggesting a luminosity slightly brighter than the Milky Way.
Digital Distance Measuring Scale Embedded Systems Project
  • Developed a digital distance measuring scale using an ultrasonic sensor, applying principles of embedded systems, hardware prototyping, and sensor interfacing.

Preprints & Publications

Preliminary Analysis of Asteroid Candidate (IASC Campaign) Pan-STARRS Data | arXiv Preprint
  • Identified a moving candidate from Pan-STARRS 1 survey data and classified it as a probable Main Belt Asteroid based on its angular velocity (~20.6 arcsec/hr).
  • Performed Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) analysis to distinguish the astronomical signal from instrumental noise.
  • Cross-verified the object using the Minor Planet Center (MPC) database, confirming it as the previously designated MBA 2021 CP66 (arXiv:2506.02963).
